Bio

I am a researcher and engineer interested in medical applications of machine learning and AI. My work focused on building computational fluid dynamics simulations of blood flow in imaging-generated 3D models of coronary arteries, as well as simulations of plaque dynamics. This research involved using AI image segmentation techniques to generate geometries for simulations.

Outside of medical research I have a strong interest in the transformative effects of AI in academic research, and more broadly in society, politics, and the economy. To explore these fields I have taken sabbatical roles in the public sector, exploring open data policies across industries, as well as a volunteer role in a community activism group tackling hate crimes during and after the COVID-19 pandemic caused by widespread media bias that affects human perception and algorithms.

I received my PhD in Medical Physics and Biomedical Engineering from University College London, where I was advised by Professor Ryo Torii and Dr. Leon Menezes. My research explored methods of improving imaging-based diagnoses of Coronary Artery Disease, specifically CT-FFR.

I am also passionate about applying cutting-edge research and technology to the real world, and have worked in the startup ecosystem, including a Y Combinator-funded startup in the healthtech space.

I currently work as a researcher in these areas, contribute as an Open Science contributor to MedARC, and am working on a startup (stealth) involving AI in academic research and education.

Annals of Biomedical Engineering Read paper

A Novel Demography-Based Approach to Define Patient-Specific Outflow Boundary Conditions in CT-Based FFR Computations

Proposes a practical demographic model for estimating patient-specific microvascular response in CT-FFR calculations without additional perfusion imaging.

Medical Engineering & Physics Read paper

On outflow boundary conditions for CT-based computation of FFR: Examination using PET images

Studies outflow boundary condition sensitivity in CT-based FFR and compares morphology-based assumptions with PET perfusion-based conditions.

Fluids Read paper

Impact of Inflow Boundary Conditions on the Calculation of CT-Based FFR

Examines how pulsatile, steady, patient-specific, and population-average inflow boundary conditions affect CT-based fractional flow reserve calculations.
